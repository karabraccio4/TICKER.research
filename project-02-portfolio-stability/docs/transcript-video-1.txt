My project asks whether a community arts nonprofit should use this portfolio strategy for money it needs to keep in reserve over the next five years. The portfolio needs to aim for at least a 5.5% yearly return and keep at least 20% in safe, reserve-eligible funds like BIL and IEF.

My recommendation is to pilot, not adopt. That means I would test the strategy with a limited allocation first instead of making it the nonprofit's permanent plan. Based on these historical inputs, I would test a range of 70 to 75% BIL and 25 to 30% VT.

The system changed in three main ways. First, it now compares my recommended portfolio to two simple alternatives: an equal-weight portfolio and an inverse-volatility portfolio. Both simple alternatives fail the 5.5% return rule. My constrained portfolio passes both the return rule and the reserve rule. This helps show why my recommendation is different from the simpler options.

On September 30, 2026, the latest known monthly reading was August at 3.63%. September's 3.75% reading was published October 1, so I excluded it. Third, I tested the return-estimate sensitivity. With the full history, the constrained mix was 75% BIL and 25% VT, with 5.51% expected return, 3.84% volatility, and 75% reserve eligible. The allocation sensitivity is why I recommend a pilot rather than adoption.
